# 01. AI Risk Management

AI governance is not paperwork after engineering. It is the set of controls that
make AI systems understandable, reviewable, and accountable.

## NIST AI RMF Functions

| Function | Practical Question |
|----------|--------------------|
| Govern | Who owns policy, risk, approval, and accountability? |
| Map | What is the use case, context, data, users, and harm model? |
| Measure | How do we evaluate quality, safety, privacy, and robustness? |
| Manage | What controls, mitigations, monitoring, and response plans exist? |

## GenAI Risk Register Fields

Use a simple register:

- Risk ID
- Use case
- Risk statement
- Risk function: govern/map/measure/manage
- Impact
- Likelihood
- Owner
- Control
- Evidence
- Review date
- Residual risk

## Governance Evidence

Useful evidence includes:

- Eval report
- Red-team results
- Prompt/model/version record
- Data lineage
- Access policy
- Observability dashboard
- Approval record
- Incident runbook
- Rollback plan

## Key Takeaways

1. Governance needs owners and evidence.
2. Risk records should link to engineering artifacts.
3. The AI lifecycle includes retirement and data deletion.
